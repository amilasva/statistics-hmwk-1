03 — Discussion and Conclusions

Deterministic mapping

The same private key and base point always produce the same public point. The mapping is deterministic.

Distribution and uniformity

The output coordinates belong to a small, restricted set of curve points. Their frequencies need not be equal. This tiny example cannot establish the statistical properties of real cryptographic systems.

One-way property

Computing Q = kG is straightforward. Recovering k from Q is the elliptic-curve discrete logarithm problem. On this toy curve, however, the small group makes exhaustive search easy.

Not a cryptographic hash function

Elliptic-curve multiplication is used in public-key cryptography and digital signatures. Unlike a cryptographic hash function, it is not designed to provide hash-specific properties such as an avalanche effect. Changing k by one does not necessarily change every output bit.

Important limitation: eight scalar values are far too few to establish uniformity, independence, or meaningful statistical correlations.
